Sheeraz Athar

Postdoctoral Researcher · PAIR Initiative, University of Notre Dame

I build robots that feel. I work with Prof. Nikolaus Correll on dexterous robotic hands — combining compliant mechanics, embedded tactile sensing, and learning so robots can handle the physical world with human-like finesse.

I received my Ph.D. from Purdue University, advised by Prof. Yu She at the MARS Lab, where I developed vision-based and fiber-based tactile sensors (VisTac, VibTac, FibTac), tactile-reactive grippers, and a trimodal underwater robot. Before that I completed an M.Phil. in Robotics at HKUST with Prof. Michael Yu Wang, and a B.E. in Mechanical Engineering at Aligarh Muslim University.

My work spans soft robotics, multimodal tactile sensing, underwater and surgical robotics, and learning-enabled perception — always validated on real hardware.

Research

I study how robots can perceive and act through physical contact. Most robots see the world but cannot feel it; I build the sensing, mechanisms, and learning pipelines that close that gap — and validate everything on real hardware, in the lab and in the field.

Dexterous hands & grasping

Sensorized, compliant hands and tactile-reactive grippers for in-hand manipulation — VTAP Gripper; current work at Notre Dame PAIR.

Multimodal tactile sensing

Vision- and fiber-based fingertips fusing geometry, force, vibration, and texture — VisTac, VibTac, MuxGel.

Soft & bio-inspired robotics

Pneumatic, auxetic, and fiber-embedded structures that deform safely and sense while they move — FibTac.

Underwater & field robotics

A patent-pending trimodal robot that drifts, glides, and thrusts — built for long-duration ocean and lake missions.

Surgical tactile sensing

Millimeter-scale sensors that bring touch to minimally invasive surgery — TacScope, MVP-Tac.
